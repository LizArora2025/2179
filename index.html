Where Sharks Strike

Understanding Shark Incidents in Australia

Australia is home to some of the world’s most famous beaches and coastlines. However, Australian waters are also home to several dangerous shark species. This visualisation explores where, when, and why shark incidents occur across Australia.

Using data from the Australian Shark-Incident Database, this project investigates shark incident trends, geographical hotspots, environmental conditions, and human behaviour associated with shark encounters.

Shark Incidents Over Time

Shark incidents in Australia have changed significantly over the years. Increasing coastal populations, tourism, and ocean recreation activities may contribute to more interactions between humans and sharks.

Which States Experience the Most Incidents?

Shark incidents are not evenly distributed across Australia. Coastal states with strong surfing and beach cultures tend to record higher numbers of incidents.

Where Do Shark Incidents Occur?

Mapping shark incidents reveals clear geographic hotspots around Australia’s coastline. Areas with high beach activity and marine biodiversity often experience more encounters.

When Are Incidents Most Common?

Shark incidents often occur during specific times of the year and at particular times of the day. Understanding these temporal patterns can improve public awareness and beach safety.

Human Behaviour and Shark Encounters

While many shark incidents are unprovoked, some occur when humans intentionally interact with sharks through activities such as fishing or handling marine animals.

Does Shark Size Affect Injury Severity?

Larger sharks are generally associated with more severe injuries. However, injury outcomes also depend on factors such as species, victim activity, and environmental conditions.

Conclusion

Shark incidents in Australia are influenced by geography, time, environmental conditions, and human behaviour. Although shark attacks remain relatively rare, understanding these patterns can help Australians make safer decisions when entering the ocean.
