Where Sharks Strike

Understanding Shark Incidents in Australia

Introduction

Australia has one of the world's longest coastlines and a strong culture of swimming, surfing and ocean recreation. Although shark incidents are relatively rare, they receive considerable public attention due to their potential severity.

Using data from the Australian Shark-Incident Database and Australian Bureau of Statistics population data, this visualisation explores how shark incidents have changed over time, where they occur, and the factors associated with shark encounters.

Shark Incidents Over Time

Shark incidents remained relatively low throughout the nineteenth century before increasing significantly during the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

Increased coastal populations, recreational ocean activities and improved reporting practices may all contribute to this trend.

Key Insight: Shark incidents have generally increased over time, with the highest annual totals occurring in recent decades.

Which States Experience the Most Incidents?

Shark incidents are concentrated in New South Wales, Queensland and Western Australia.

These states possess long coastlines, active surfing communities and large coastal populations.

Key Insight: NSW, QLD and WA account for the majority of shark incidents.

Where Do Shark Incidents Occur?

The map highlights clear shark incident hotspots along Australia's eastern and western coastlines.

Many incidents occur near heavily used recreational beaches and surfing destinations.

Key Insight: Shark incidents are heavily concentrated near major coastal population centres.

When Are Incidents Most Common?

Shark incidents are not evenly distributed throughout the year or day.

Certain periods show noticeably higher incident frequencies, suggesting links with environmental conditions and human activity.

Key Insight: Shark incidents display clear temporal patterns rather than occurring randomly.

Provoked vs Unprovoked Incidents

Most incidents recorded in Australia are classified as unprovoked encounters.

Provoked incidents typically involve fishing, handling sharks or direct interaction with marine wildlife.

Key Insight: The majority of shark incidents are unprovoked.

Does Shark Size Affect Injury Severity?

Larger sharks are often associated with more severe injuries, although many other factors influence incident outcomes.

Species behaviour, victim activity and environmental conditions can all affect severity.

Key Insight: Larger sharks tend to be linked with more severe outcomes.

Conclusion

Shark incidents in Australia are influenced by geography, population distribution, environmental conditions and human behaviour.

Although incidents have increased over time, they remain relatively rare compared with the millions of people who enter Australian waters each year.
